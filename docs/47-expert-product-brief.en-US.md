# MajorPilot Product Concept Design Brief

MajorPilot Team | October 16, 2026

We want to design a product for the U.S. market that helps people choose among master's programs. It should help them understand the work they would actually study and do, try a small part of it if useful, and decide what to investigate or prepare for next.

We are inviting you to develop your own product and experience concept, including core features, user journeys, visual direction, and the first set of subject experiences. We want to see how you would design this product. The ideas in this brief are open to discussion; you do not need to follow our existing screens, tasks, or technical implementation.

## 1 The people and decisions we want to support

### People with an unresolved question

Our initial audience is college juniors and seniors, recent graduates, and early-career professionals comparing master's programs. They have some fields or programs in mind but are unsure what the coursework involves or what foundations they would need to build. They may be comparing related fields or considering a change of discipline. We should not assume prior study in the field they want to explore.

A typical question is: "I'm considering Data Science and Business Analytics. Both mention data analysis, but what would I actually do in each? Which kind of work should I explore further?" People who have already decided and only need help with application logistics are not our initial priority.

### Make the coursework easier to understand

Users should see concrete activities, examples of completed work, and credible sources, not just course titles and program descriptions. They should leave able to explain how the work differs, what remains unclear, and what to check or try next. If the materials answer their question, they should not have to complete a task for the visit to be useful.

The product must distinguish an academic field, a specific university's master's program, and an illustrative task. One case shows one type of work. It cannot represent an entire field or every program with a similar name.

### Product boundaries

MajorPilot informs a choice; it does not make it for the user, predict admission, or turn one experience into an ability ranking or a "fit score." We are not aiming to build a full online course, a general-purpose AI chatbot, or an application management service.

The U.S. is the target market for this concept and subsequent validation. Demand and willingness to pay are not yet established. We first need to show that the product adds enough understanding to justify the time, compared with reading university websites, talking to current students, or using general-purpose AI.

## 2 An experience that starts with a question

Users should control how deeply they engage. Our starting idea is to help them explore coursework and examples, try a task if useful, and make sense of what they learn about their options. The needs below do not prescribe a screen sequence, session length, or interaction pattern.

| User need | What the product should enable | What to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clarify the comparison | Capture candidate fields or programs, prior knowledge, and open questions. Suggest a starting point with a reason, and allow a different choice. | A lengthy intake form or a score based on background. |
| Understand the work | Connect course sources and prerequisites to typical activities and a readable example of completed work. | Promotional descriptions alone, or presenting an illustrative example as an actual course assignment. |
| Decide whether to try a task | Explain the question it can help explore, the tools needed, and the approximate effort. Allow a summary based on materials alone. | Requiring every task to be completed before offering anything useful. |
| Try with support | Make the activity understandable to someone new to the field and offer help when needed. | Unexplained terminology that blocks progress, or making every judgment for the user. |
| Pause and return | Save materials, responses, and progress. Allow a switch to another field and a return to the original experience. | Losing work after a refresh or a change of direction. |
| Identify a next step | Provide an evidence-based recap, unresolved questions, and preparation suggestions. Compare options when useful. | Scores alone, generic encouragement, or unexplained recommendations. |

### Course information must be traceable

Claims about a specific program should link to official course information, prerequisites, or other clearly identified material, with the relevant program and source date shown. Users should be able to distinguish university content, our interpretation, and teaching examples. Do not extend a source beyond what it supports. Where information is missing, say so and allow exploration at the field level.

The concept does not need to cover every university. Propose a manageable starting scope and explain how materials would be selected, reviewed, and updated. Do not rely on real-time AI to generate extensive program descriptions that appear complete but cannot be checked.

### Accounts should help users save their work

Let people understand the product and see useful content before asking them to create an account to save or continue. You can choose the timing, but do not hide a promised result behind registration. Collect only what is needed for the immediate purpose. Transcripts, resumes, and complete application materials should not be prerequisites for an experience.

## 3 Initial fields and experience combinations

Our tentative starting fields are Artificial Intelligence (AI), Data Science (DS), and Business Analytics (BA). We envision two ways to explore them:

- Standalone experiences: one each for AI, DS, and BA, for someone interested in a single field.
- Comparative experiences: AI versus DS and DS versus BA, to help people understand the differences between fields.

This is not a fixed content plan. If a different set of three fields would make a better starting point, please propose it, explain why, and suggest the corresponding comparisons. A comparative experience does not have to consist of completing two standalone tasks in sequence.

## 4 Experience content and outcomes are yours to design

We have not prescribed case topics, methods, question formats, tools, instructional steps, or a report structure. Please propose the experience and way of presenting results that you think best serve the product's purpose.

What matters is whether someone without a background in the field can understand and participate, and whether the experience clarifies their choice. Feedback should have a concrete basis and make clear what the experience does and does not establish. It need not take the form of a long report.

## 5 The interface experience we want

We want a distinctive product that people take seriously and would recommend to a peer. It should not look like a generic admin dashboard. Please propose the colors, typography, layout, graphics, and motion. The homepage should make the value clear without relying on unverified partnerships or outcome promises to establish trust.

Product copy should use natural American English for people new to the field, with unfamiliar terms explained. Users should be able to find the next step, get help, and pause or resume easily. When something goes wrong, they should understand what happened and how to continue. We are not prescribing specific screens, navigation, or feedback patterns.

Consider use across devices and explain the situations the product is designed for. Personal content should be private by default. Users should understand how their information is used, control sharing, and be able to request deletion. If your concept uses AI, explain its role and limitations clearly.

## 6 Documents for this engagement

Please provide a product concept design document explaining your approach to MajorPilot, covering:

1. **Product positioning and feature scope.** Your interpretation of the intended users, the core problem, and the features needed.
2. **User journeys.** How people begin, learn about and try a field, and receive something useful for their decision.
3. **Subject experiences and comparisons.** Your proposed initial fields and approach to standalone and comparative experiences.
4. **Interface and results presentation.** The visual direction, information organization, and interaction approach, including the feedback or decision support users would receive.

Use written explanations, with flow diagrams or illustrations where helpful. The document structure is up to you. Prototypes will be commissioned separately; this stage does not require clickable prototypes, high-fidelity screens, or software implementation.
